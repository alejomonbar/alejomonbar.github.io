I am a postdoctoral researcher at the [Jülich Supercomputing Centre (JSC)](https://www.fz-juelich.de/en/ias/jsc) in Germany, where I bridge the worlds of quantum computing and high-performance computing. My research explores how quantum devices—both analog and digital—can tackle complex combinatorial optimization problems, and I use some of Europe's most powerful supercomputers to simulate and benchmark quantum systems across different platforms.

My recent breakthrough work on **Linear-Ramp QAOA (LR-QAOA)** demonstrates that fixed parameter schedules can achieve near-optimal solutions across diverse combinatorial optimization problems, successfully tested on quantum processors with up to **109 qubits**. This research, published in *npj Quantum Information* and *Quantum Information Processing*, provides evidence of scaling advantages in quantum optimization and has been validated on hardware from IBM, IonQ, Rigetti, Quantinuum, and D-Wave.

Beyond research, I'm passionate about building open-source tools that make quantum computing more accessible. As an **OpenQAOA SDK maintainer** and contributor to Qiskit, PennyLane, and D-Wave Ocean, I help bridge the gap between theoretical algorithms and practical implementations. My work was recognized with a Unitary Fund grant and multiple QHack competition wins (2022, 2023).

My journey started with a B.Sc. in electromechanical engineering from the Pedagogical and Technological University of Colombia (UPTC), followed by both M.Sc. and Ph.D. (Summa cum laude) degrees in mechanical engineering from the University of Guanajuato. With **16+ publications** spanning quantum computing, optimization, and machine learning, this multidisciplinary background gives me a unique perspective on solving real-world challenges.
