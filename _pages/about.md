I am a postdoctoral researcher at the [Jülich Supercomputing Centre (JSC)](https://www.fz-juelich.de/en/ias/jsc) in Germany, where I bridge the worlds of quantum computing and high-performance computing. My research explores how quantum devices—both analog and digital—can tackle complex combinatorial optimization problems, and I use some of Europe's most powerful supercomputers to simulate and benchmark quantum systems across different platforms.

Beyond research, I'm passionate about building open-source tools that make quantum computing more accessible. I've contributed to major quantum software libraries including Qiskit, PennyLane, D-Wave Ocean, and OpenQAOA. My work on OpenQAOA was recognized with a grant from the Unitary Fund to advance combinatorial optimization applications in quantum computing.

My journey started with a B.Sc. in electromechanical engineering from the Pedagogical and Technological University of Colombia (UPTC), followed by both M.Sc. and Ph.D. (Summa cum laude) degrees in mechanical engineering from the University of Guanajuato. This multidisciplinary background—spanning classical engineering, computational physics, and quantum computing—gives me a unique perspective on solving real-world optimization challenges.
